## The customer

A global technology manufacturing group operates in semiconductor displays, photovoltaic energy, and smart devices. At the time of the published case, it employed more than 130,000 people and served over 160 countries and regions. The company is not named in the source.

Its financial shared services center processes documents and reviews expenses for the wider group. As international operations expanded, that centralized team had to accommodate many more languages and document formats.

## The challenge: a domestic workflow meets global documents

The center handles more than eight million documents annually and approximately 2,000 submission requests per day. A request can contain more than ten types of supporting document.

The previous reimbursement system worked well with standardized domestic invoices. It struggled with overseas invoices, retail receipts, travel records, event proposals, expense lists, payment evidence, delivery notes, warehouse receipts, and itineraries. In total, the workflow needed to support more than 50 document types.

![An overseas VAT invoice used in the original case](/images/cases/20260709164632354/figure-1.webp)

Low recognition accuracy left staff entering fields and reviewing attachments manually. Only about 50% of expense reviews passed automatically. More than 300 reviewers supported the process, while overseas employees faced long reimbursement cycles.

The replacement also had to run within the group's private environment to meet its data security and compliance requirements. Handling month-end and quarter-end peaks was essential.

## The solution: one document engine behind financial shared services

After nearly a year of discussions and comparative testing, the group selected INTSIG DocFlow.

### Extract across languages and layouts

DocFlow covers more than 50 document types in this implementation. The team's evaluation included multilingual invoices, nonstandard layouts, watermarks, long tables, documents without clear type labels, and pages containing several attached receipts.

The resulting structured data reduces the number of documents that require manual entry before review.

![Invoice fields extracted in the original DocFlow interface](/images/cases/20260709164632354/figure-2.webp)

### Keep processing stable through peak periods

The service supports the center's daily submission volume and annual document workload. Its concurrent processing architecture is used to manage month-end and quarter-end peaks and keep the financial review workflow operating continuously.

### Connect capture, review, and posting

The private deployment integrates with the group's existing financial systems. Documents move from collection through recognition, classification, and field extraction into the automated review and accounting process.

![The original case diagram showing the integrated financial document workflow](/images/cases/20260709164632354/figure-3.webp)

## Reported results

| Measure | Published result |
| --- | --- |
| Automated expense review pass rate | Increased from approximately 50% to 70% |
| Overall document recognition rate | More than 85% |
| Intelligent document processing | Completed within six hours in the reported workflow |
| Submission and approval | Completed within one day in the reported workflow |
| Reviewer staffing target | A planned reduction from more than 300 reviewers to 100 |

The staffing figure is a target, rather than a completed reduction. The source also reports stable processing during business peaks and an improved reimbursement experience for overseas employees.

For the shared services team, the main change is wider automation coverage: international and nonstandard documents can enter the same financial workflow as standardized domestic invoices.

*Results are reported in the original customer case and reflect that implementation at publication.*
