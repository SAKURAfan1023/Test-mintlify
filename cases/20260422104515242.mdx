## The customer

A specialized cargo carrier within a major Chinese shipping group transports heavy equipment, construction machinery, wind power equipment, pulp, vehicles, rail rolling stock, livestock, and refrigerated goods. The source case keeps the company anonymous.

## The challenge: every agent sends a different layout

Daily operations involve cargo manifests, bills of lading, and statements of facts (SOFs) in Word, PDF, Excel, scans, and images. Customers and agents use different layouts and field descriptions.

The company’s in-house document system previously combined deep learning models with robotic process automation. Fixed-layout models required frequent retraining and rule changes. Staff still entered data and checked consistency across documents, while the company’s internal agent platform could produce unstable interpretations of complex paperwork.

An SOF records events such as arrival, berthing, and loading or unloading. These times inform demurrage and despatch calculations. At one port, staff took an average of 39 minutes to process a two- or three-page SOF, including reading contract terms and manually organizing the information.

## The solution: extraction inside the shipping workflow

After a proof of concept, the company selected DocFlow as the processing engine behind its existing document system. The implementation supports different customer and agent layouts without a separate template for each one.

### Extract the fields operations needs

For bills of lading, the workflow extracts 14 categories of information, including shipper, consignee, notify party, vessel and voyage, ports, bill of lading number, quantity, weight, and volume.

For SOFs, it extracts arrival, berthing, start-of-handling, and completion times. The business system uses these events in its laytime and charge calculations.

### Connect documents to the full process

The integration covers upload, parsing, field extraction, cross-document comparison, and workflow actions. It supports operations from booking and packing through customs declaration, loading, settlement, and clearance.

## What the two-port pilot showed

| Measure | Published pilot result |
| --- | --- |
| Jobs processed | 493 |
| Working time saved | 238.3 hours |
| SOF handling time at one port | 39 minutes reduced to 5–10 minutes |
| Field recognition on evaluated samples | 60–70% previously; above 90% with DocFlow |

The source reports approximately 4–8× improvement for the SOF task and more than 10× improvement across the broader document process. These describe different scopes; the overall figure should not be presented as the timing result for every individual SOF.

The team also reduced the engineering effort spent maintaining templates and rules. The practical change was a workflow that could accept varied documents, compare the extracted information, and return usable data to operations.

The results come from a two-port pilot. A rollout to additional document types, ports, or languages should be validated against its own samples and business rules.

<p className="case-results-note">Results are reported in the original customer case. They reflect that implementation at publication and may differ in other environments.</p>
