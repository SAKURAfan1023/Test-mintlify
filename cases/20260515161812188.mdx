## The customer

A major construction machinery group is bringing AI and data capabilities into research, manufacturing, commercial operations, and customer services. Its international operations depend on large collections of technical and business documents. The company is not named in the published case.

## The challenge: many workflows, one unstructured data barrier

Different teams were encountering related document problems:

- **Engineering:** drawings contain borders and lines that conventional recognition tools can confuse with tables, affecting downstream cost analysis.
- **International finance and service:** overseas invoices vary widely, while repair manuals contain dense tables, images, and multi-level sections that must remain searchable and traceable.
- **Contract review:** staff check seals and signatures across multi-page, nonstandard contracts, including text obscured by stamps.
- **Audit:** business units submit management reports in different formats, making manual analysis slow and important risks easy to overlook.
- **Knowledge management:** formulas, multiple columns, and complex layouts were being lost or scrambled before documents reached the knowledge base.

![An engineering drawing from the original case](/images/cases/20260515161812188/figure-1.webp)

## The solution: xParse as a platform service

The group selected TextIn xParse as a common parsing engine for unstructured documents across its business systems.

### Preserve structure and source elements

xParse parses engineering drawings, invoices, and technical manuals while retaining headings, paragraphs, and document hierarchy. It labels text, images, and other elements so downstream applications can select content and prepare knowledge chunks.

![The engineering drawing and its structured parsing output](/images/cases/20260515161812188/figure-2.webp)

### Reconstruct dense tables across pages

Repair documentation often contains long tables spanning several pages. The implementation preserves row and column relationships and associates cells with the correct headers. Service applications use that structured data to retrieve fault codes and repair instructions.

![A dense multi-page technical table and the original extraction interface](/images/cases/20260515161812188/figure-3.webp)

### Support contract checks and existing AI applications

The deployment includes seal detection and recognition of text beneath stamps to assist contract review. Business applications continue to own review logic and decisions.

Standard APIs and private deployment connect the parsing service to departmental systems and workflow platforms such as Dify. Structured outputs supply downstream field extraction, retrieval-augmented generation, and internal AI assistants.

## Reported results

### International procurement and finance

Invoice approval time fell from two minutes to one minute. The reported error rate decreased from 1.2% to 0.2%.

At an annual volume of 40,000 invoices, the group projected savings of approximately 80 person-days and a CNY 600,000 annual reduction in risk costs associated with duplicate reimbursement. These are projections in the source, rather than realized savings verified by this article.

### Overseas maintenance

System retrieval time fell from 13 seconds to five seconds, a reduction of more than 60%. The wider task of finding fault codes and repair information moved from hours to minutes in the reported service workflow.

### Sales and marketing support

| Measure | Published result |
| --- | --- |
| Frontline information retrieval efficiency | Improved by 70% |
| Cross-department collaboration efficiency | Improved by 50% |
| Proposal preparation efficiency | Improved by 60% |
| Share of high-quality content generated with AI | More than 40% |
| Per-person productivity | Improved by 43% |

The source also reports faster resolution of customer questions and improved satisfaction in these workflows.

### Internal audit

Management report analysis efficiency improved by 70%. Average audit task duration decreased from four to five hours to less than 30 minutes, while the reported identification rate for key risks reached 98%.

The common parsing layer lets the group apply document capabilities across departments without rebuilding the same input pipeline for each new AI workflow.
