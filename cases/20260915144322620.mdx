## The customer

A regional commercial bank in China serves small and medium-sized businesses and local residents through corporate banking, retail banking, international settlement, and lending services. The published case does not disclose the bank’s name.

## The challenge: fragmented processing across business lines

Branch operations, credit teams, international settlement teams, and the image archive all handled large volumes of forms, contracts, vouchers, and nonstandard documents. Staff entered fields, checked results, classified files, and organized pages manually.

Entering a single branch voucher typically took 15–20 seconds. At peak times, those repetitive tasks became an operational bottleneck. Historical image archives introduced another problem: incorrectly uploaded documents, mixed file types, and pages stored out of order were difficult to identify through manual spot checks.

Separate OCR implementations across business lines also duplicated investment. Adding a new contract, invoice, or approval format often required another adaptation project.

## The solution: embed DocFlow behind existing systems

The bank deployed INTSIG DocFlow within its private environment and connected it through APIs to four core systems: branch operations, the image platform, international settlement, and credit management. Frontline staff continued using their established applications.

![Illustrated banking document workflow](/images/diagrams/banking.svg)

### Recognition and extraction for each business line

- **Branch operations:** read application forms, cash vouchers, and transfer checks, then write extracted values back to the business system.
- **International settlement:** process letters of credit, bills of lading, overseas invoices, and customs declarations across different layouts and languages.
- **Credit review:** structure information from contracts, insurance policies, and foreign exchange approval documents to support review.

A shared platform provides recognition, classification, extraction, and document comparison. Configurable and model-assisted extraction reduces the need for separately labeled datasets and custom training for every new format.

### Archive governance as a recurring workflow

DocFlow classifies incoming documents and helps organize multi-page image files. Scheduled overnight checks identify problematic historical uploads and generate correction lists for the responsible teams. This changes archive governance from occasional spot checks into a repeatable operational process.

## Reported results

| Measure | Published result |
| --- | --- |
| Voucher entry time | 15–20 seconds reduced to 5–7 seconds |
| Reported voucher entry improvement | Approximately 60% |
| Connected core systems | Four |
| Production records processed | More than 10,000, including over 8,000 in operations |
| Historical records processed in testing | More than 50,000 |

The production and test volumes are distinct. The figures describe the deployment at the time of publication, rather than current platform-wide totals.

Automatic page ordering also reduced repetitive preparation work for customer managers. Overnight archive inspection gave the bank a more systematic way to find upload and compliance issues while retaining a correction workflow for human owners.

The case illustrates a platform approach: document capabilities operate inside existing banking systems, with shared infrastructure supporting several teams.

<p className="case-results-note">Results are reported in the original customer case. They reflect that implementation at publication and may differ in other environments.</p>
